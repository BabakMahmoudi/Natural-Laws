# Causation as a Working Assumption

*AI-generated draft (model: GPT-6.1); developed from [the dialogue with Babak Mahmoudi](../Dialogs/2026-10-30-kant-causation-and-predictive-world.md). Not yet reviewed or endorsed by Babak.*

Kant's Second Analogy proposes that we can judge an objective succession of events only by bringing it under a causal rule. Babak's question is whether a condition for making sense of experience also establishes causal necessity in the physical world. Dreams make the distinction pressing: an apparent event may be experienced without an evident cause within the dream, although a dream may temporarily seem like a coherent world.

Babak proposes that a creature maintains a persistent model of objects and events, revising it against sensory input. Predictive coding offers one empirical account of how predictions and mismatches might contribute to such revision. On this proposal, causal expectations help the model anticipate events and guide action. Its practical success gives the creature reason to trust that the model tracks reliable relationships in the world; it does not alone establish that governing necessities of the same kind exist independently of the model.

The AI's qualification is that dreams, too, can exhibit order. What matters for this comparison may be the waking model's sustained correction through perception and action, rather than perfect predictability or an absolute absence of order in dreams. Nor does the empirical predictive-coding proposal prove Kant's transcendental argument: a proposed neural mechanism and a claim about the necessary conditions of objective experience have different burdens of proof.

The question left open is whether causal organization is *a priori* necessary for any objective experience, as Kant argues, or a powerful and revisable assumption whose authority is earned in practice. Further inquiry should examine what counts as successful correlation between model and world without treating that success as proof that laws govern events beyond experience.
