# Laws as Descriptions of a Complete History

*Source dialogue: [The Law and the Shape of a World](../Dialogs/2026-10-29-law-and-shape-of-a-world.md).*

Babak asks what a law of nature could be besides a statement about the world. His recreation example sharpens the question: if someone copies two particles and their motions, must he copy a law as an additional ingredient? In a block-time picture, the assignment is to reproduce the particles' entire trajectories, past and future. Once that complete history is copied, constant velocity is a feature of its shape, available for concise description; no separate rule appears to require copying.

The check at an intermediate time matters. Matching the current positions and all recorded past positions does not verify that the future trajectories match. We can wait for later observations or imagine inspecting the complete block, but examining a supposed law at the intermediate moment cannot replace that check. Babak's blind man on the stairs illustrates the epistemic role of laws: a description of the pattern helps an observer anticipate what remains unseen without making the next step occur.

The emerging proposal is that the universe has a structured history that unfolds *for our experience*, while a law describes a recurring pattern or symmetry of that history. The AI related this proposal to Humean accounts, especially Lewis's best-system view, and to block-time perspectives; those comparisons are interpretive pointers, not an identification of Babak's proposal with any complete doctrine. “Symmetry” may also be narrower than the full range of lawful descriptions.

The central open question is whether reproducing the complete history leaves out any real feature that a governing-law account could identify. A realist may maintain that an underlying structure explains why the trajectories have their form. The recreation argument asks what that structure adds to an exact copy; it does not establish that no such explanation exists. Why this history has its particular shape remains outside the present inquiry.
