# Causation and the Predictive World

*AI-generated dialogue (model: GPT-6.1); developed from a conversation with Babak Mahmoudi on 30 October 2026. Edited and condensed for readability; not yet reviewed or endorsed by Babak. The speakers' claims retain their sequence, while some passages are reconstructed rather than verbatim.*

**Babak:** I am curious about Kant's account of natural laws and causation, especially his claim that Newton's laws of motion are a priori.

**GPT-6.1:** Kant distinguishes a general condition of objective experience from particular empirical laws. In the Second Analogy, he argues that judging an objective alteration requires a rule determining the order of states. In the *Metaphysical Foundations*, he tries to derive principles of mechanics, including inertia and reciprocal action, from the concept of matter as movable in space. He does not derive the precise inverse-square law of gravitation by pure thought.

**Babak:** Does he mean causation is required for any experience? Dreams are experiences, and events in them can arise without causes.

**GPT-6.1:** Dreams show that sensations and apparent events need not have causes within the dream's story. Kant's narrower claim concerns judging a succession to be an event of an object in an objective world. But your example presses a difficulty: a dream may seem objective while it lasts. What establishes that a judgment succeeds rather than merely feels convincing?

**Babak:** What does he mean by *judging*? I distinguish the physical world, human experience, and our descriptions and explanations. A person may need a coherent story to make sense of experience. Why should that necessitate causation in the physical realm?

**GPT-6.1:** To judge, here, is to take a succession as belonging to an object: “the boat moved,” rather than “I perceived the boat here and then there.” Kant's claim applies to objects of possible experience, not things as they are independently of any possible experience. The necessity of causal organization in our judgment alone does not prove causal necessity in a wholly mind-independent realm.

**Babak:** We have access to visual, tactile, and auditory data. We construct a theory of persistent objects as their sources. This works only if there is a reasonable, lawlike relationship between sensory data and physical objects. In that world of possible experience, causation is a prerequisite. Is that close?

**GPT-6.1:** Close, though Kant would not usually say that ordinary objects are hidden causes inferred from prior sensory data. The boat is an object of experience, constituted as such through the organization of appearances. His causal rule primarily relates objective states and events, not a sensory datum to an inaccessible thing. Still, your account makes vivid why a stable, intelligible world matters.

**Babak:** Kant could have gone further if he had known theories in which the brain maintains a forecast model of the world and corrects it with sensory data. What are they called?

**GPT-6.1:** Predictive processing, with predictive coding as a proposed mechanism. Higher levels predict incoming activity, mismatches or prediction errors help revise the model, and confidence affects how strongly a mismatch counts. Active inference also considers how action changes the sensory input. These are empirical proposals whose neural details remain contested.

**Babak:** Elaborate on predictive coding.

**GPT-6.1:** Consider a cup partly hidden behind a book. The model anticipates the cup's continuation; uncovering it tests that anticipation. “Prediction” can concern present sensory activity, not only a future event. In one influential scheme, feedback carries predictions downward and residual errors travel upward. A model of persistent objects can thus remain stable while being corrected. None of this, by itself, establishes Kant's claim that causation is an a priori condition of all objective experience.

**Babak:** The internal model could be what Kant means by objective experience. It persists in a way dreams do not. Its objects and events are assumed to be causally related predictably. The model is useful and vital to survival, so the creature has good reason to trust a reliable correlation with the physical world. But that does not establish the same governing laws outside the model. Let us focus on this practical, useful assumption that works.

**GPT-6.1:** That gives the inquiry a direction. Dreams may themselves contain regularities, so the contrast needs care: a waking model remains answerable to sustained sensory correction and action across time. Successful engagement warrants trust in reliable relationships, without establishing a metaphysics of governing necessity. The open question for Kant is why a condition of a successful, revisable model should be necessary a priori rather than an empirically vindicated way of living in the world.
