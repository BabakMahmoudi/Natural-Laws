# The Law and the Shape of a World

*Edited from the conversation of 29 October 2026. The exchange is lightly condensed for readability; the creator and blind-man examples retain Babak's sequence of thought. Literature references entered only when they arose at the end of the conversation.*

**Babak:** What is a law, seriously? What could I point to in the world and say, “There is a law of nature”? I can show you Newton's laws written on paper. What can a law be other than a statement?

**GPT-6:** The paper bears an inscription, and different inscriptions can express the same claim. The harder question is what gives a claim the status of a law of nature. A realist may say it describes some further relation or structure in nature. But then we may ask what that structure is and how we identify it.

**Babak:** Regardless of what differentiates laws from other statements, on this view laws are statements, descriptions. There are things in the world and statements about them. Some statements turn out to be laws.

**GPT-6:** Yes, that is a definite philosophical position. A realist can instead argue that statements express laws existing independently of us. The challenge is to say what such laws are.

**Babak:** Imagine someone assigned to recreate a part of the universe: a container with particles A and B moving at constant velocities. He creates the particles and gives them the corresponding velocities. What must he do next? In our universe the velocities remain constant. How does he copy that law?

**GPT-6:** Giving particles velocities at one moment does not alone specify later moments. In a simulation he might implement an update rule; in a physical world he must arrange for the behavior to continue. The question is whether that arrangement requires a further governing law or simply results in a history we can describe by one.

**Babak:** In a block-time universe, the trajectories from (t_1) to (t_n) already exist. He can copy them. No rule needs copying. The law is a concise description of the trajectory's shape.

**GPT-6:** That corrects my assumption that the creator begins at (t_1) and waits for a future to unfold. Given a complete block, constant velocity is a feature of the paths through it. A realist might still posit an underlying structure explaining their shape, but must say what that adds to the structured history.

**Babak:** Then theories of laws depend on our theory of time. If past, present, and future coexist, we have a geometry of existence, though its shape has an interestingly lawful form.

**GPT-6:** A block view makes the descriptive account natural, though it does not prove it. The question of whether something explains the geometry remains open.

**Babak:** Suppose the creator returns at (t_m) and says the assignment is complete. We see A at (a_m) and B at (b_m). We might inspect records of earlier positions. But how can we check the future part of his claim? He could have copied every past position and merely promised that future ones will match. If we ask what law he copied, there is nothing additional to inspect. To point to our universe, one should point to the structure containing its whole history and future. The laws describe its symmetries.

**GPT-6:** At (t_m), the future claim cannot be verified just by checking present positions and past records. We must wait or somehow inspect the complete block. If every position and trajectory in the full history matches, asking for a separately copied law needs justification. “Symmetries” may be too narrow for every lawlike pattern, but your challenge to an additional copyable law remains.

**Babak:** In this situation, what places A at (a_m) is the creator's will, not a law of motion. The laws describe the geometry of that will. A blind man climbing stairs gathers information about the place's form to plan his next step.

**GPT-6:** The analogy clarifies the practical use of a description. The blind man uses the pattern of steps encountered to anticipate what he cannot yet see. The pattern does not push him upward. Similarly, successful prediction alone does not establish that a law pushes events along. Your appeal to the creator's will belongs to the imagined recreation; it need not settle what, if anything, explains the original universe.

**Babak:** Thus far, we understand the universe as an interesting shape with specific symmetries unfolding before human experience. Laws describe those symmetries. Why those symmetries exist is outside our current scope.

**GPT-6:** Then the claim reached here is limited: describing the world's pattern and explaining why the world has that pattern are different tasks.

**Babak:** Has anyone in the literature held something similar?

**GPT-6:** The closest family is Humean descriptivism. Hume questioned our perception of necessary connections; Ramsey and Lewis treat laws as privileged generalizations in a system describing the world's facts. Lewis's mosaic and best-system account come especially close to the complete-history idea, though they add criteria for selecting laws. Huw Price's view from “nowhen” is relevant to your block-time perspective, but his central subject is temporal and causal asymmetry. Tim Maudlin offers a contrasting view: laws are primitive features of reality. Calling your argument a “block-universe version of Humean descriptivism” is my classification, not a doctrine you have adopted wholesale.
