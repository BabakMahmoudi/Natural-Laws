## Purpose
Investigate the ontology of physical laws, starting with Humean regularity theory and David Lewis's best-system analysis.

## 1. Humean regularities
We distinguished patterns of events from any additional governing necessity. The challenge is to distinguish accidental generalizations (such as all observed gold spheres being under one kilometer in diameter) from genuine laws.

## 2. Lewis's best-system analysis
Laws are axioms or theorems of the deductive system that achieves the best balance of **simplicity** and **strength** in describing the total history of the world. Strength concerns informative coverage, not necessarily causal explanation. The ideal system is not merely the best theory currently known. Higher-level laws may be theorems rather than fundamental axioms.

## 3. Perfectly natural properties
Without restrictions on predicates, arbitrary complex histories can be disguised as simple laws by inventing predicates that encode the entire history. Example: define Z for each particle as its actual trajectory minus its actual trajectory, then 'Z = 0' is trivially true. Lewis appeals to objective, perfectly natural properties to constrain simplicity. 'Apple' is not plausibly perfectly natural in the way a fundamental physical property might be.

## 4. Five falling apples
Thought experiment: three red apples on one tree and two green apples on another fall. Five singular facts can be compressed into 'red apples fall,' 'green apples fall,' and 'apples fall.' We questioned the move from 'these apples fell' to 'apples fall' and, more fundamentally, why five distinct molecular aggregates should be grouped under the predicate 'apple.' A best-system judgment concerns the entire world, not just the five observations.

## 5. Central philosophical objection: language dependence
The user's central concern: laws in Lewis's theory are propositions in a best deductive system; simplicity and strength appear to characterize **representations**, not the physical world independently of descriptions. Different vocabularies and predicate choices can alter apparent simplicity. If no language or mathematical representation existed in a prehuman universe, in what sense could the best system or its propositions exist? Lewis can say that the world's mosaic fixes which propositions would be laws even if no one formulates them, and distinguish propositions from linguistic sentences. Yet this leaves the metaphysics of propositions, privileged properties, and language-independent simplicity contested.

A tentative distinction emerged: objective resemblance between events may be in the world, whereas simplicity may belong to their descriptions. The user was not convinced that Lewis successfully removes language dependence from the ontology of laws.

## 6. Counterfactuals
Laws seem to support claims about what would happen under unrealized conditions, unlike accidental generalizations. Lewis analyzes a counterfactual by looking to the closest possible worlds where its antecedent holds. 'Closest' means overall similarity, not physical distance.

For a deterministic world in which a person did not drop an apple, a world in which they did may require either a different prior history or a small localized divergence ('miracle') from actual laws. In Lewis's familiar ranking for standard deterministic cases, avoiding large widespread law violations matters greatly; matching particular facts matters; avoiding small law violations also matters; approximate resemblance is lower priority. The ranking is contextual rather than a universal mechanical metric.

We considered an apparent circularity: the actual world's best-system laws influence closeness rankings, while those rankings determine counterfactual truth. Lewis can order the account by grounding actual laws in the actual mosaic first, but the philosophical question remains why descriptive regularities should receive special priority in counterfactual comparison.

## Open questions
- Can simplicity be objective independently of language or representation?
- How are perfectly natural properties identified without presupposing a preferred theory?
- What ontological status do propositions and the best system have in a universe without thinkers?
- Can Humean regularities ground counterfactuals without introducing necessity through similarity judgments?
