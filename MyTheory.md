# My Theory: Current Commitments

*AI-generated synthesis (model: GPT-6-sol) of positions developed in project dialogues, memos, and essays. This is a provisional record for Babak's review, not a statement he has endorsed item by item.*

This file records our current philosophical commitments in short propositions. They are revisable claims, not established scientific facts or a completed theory. The linked writings explain the arguments and their limits.

1. **The world exhibits an ordered history of events.** We encounter that history as unfolding; a block-time account treats it as a complete structure. Our descriptive proposal is clearest under the latter picture, but does not require that picture to be settled here. [Dialogue](Dialogs/2026-10-29-law-and-shape-of-a-world.md) · [Memo](Memos/Laws-as-Descriptions-of-a-Complete-History.md)

2. **A statement of a natural law describes a pattern or constraint in the world.** Its usefulness in calculation and prediction does not, by itself, show that the statement is an additional entity governing events. [Dialogue](Dialogs/2026-10-29-law-and-shape-of-a-world.md) · [Essay](Essays/Copying-a-Universe-and-the-Place-of-Laws.md)

3. **Prediction and causation are indispensable working frameworks.** Their practical success warrants using them and trusting reliable relationships, without alone proving that governing necessities exist independently of our descriptions. [Memo](Memos/2026-10-30-causation-as-working-assumption.md) · [Essay](Essays/2026-10-30-the-world-we-keep-in-view.md)

4. **An epistemic difficulty does not settle an ontological question.** Difficulty distinguishing a law from an accidental regularity does not establish a separate property of lawhood; neither does this argument prove that no such property exists. [Dialogue](Dialogs/Laws-Accidents-and-Events.md) · [Memo](Memos/Accidental-Regularities-and-the-Ontology-of-Laws.md)

5. **A complete-history test asks what a law adds to an exact copy.** If all events and relations are copied, a defender of governing laws owes an account of the further feature allegedly missing. Matching only the past at an intermediate time is a different, continuation test. [Memo](Memos/Laws-as-Descriptions-of-a-Complete-History.md) · [Essay](Essays/Copying-a-Universe-and-the-Place-of-Laws.md)

6. **Our classifications depend partly on representation.** The simplicity and strength of a proposed system of laws are assessed through a vocabulary and choice of properties. Whether an objective best system can overcome this dependence remains open. [Dialogue](Dialogs/2026-10-17-hume-lewis-best-system-counterfactuals.md) · [Memo](Memos/Accidental-Regularities-and-the-Ontology-of-Laws.md)

7. **Derived regularities need not be counted as additional primitive laws.** This does not yet decide whether any primitive constraints, powers, or modal facts belong to the world's ontology. [Imagined Maudlin dialogue](Dialogs/2026-10-31-an-imagined-conversation-with-tim-maudlin.md) · [Imagined Bird dialogue](Dialogs/2026-11-01-an-imagined-conversation-with-alexander-bird.md)

- Why does the world's history have the particular order it has? This question is currently outside the scope of the descriptive proposal.
